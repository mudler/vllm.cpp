**A correction to this report's own first draft, which a fresh review caught.**
That draft said PORTQ-1's date-monotonicity check "does not survive into this
tranche", because two rendered dates invert at positions 95 and 116, and it
attributed those inversions to `git rev-list --reverse` emitting reverse
*topological* order under merge topology. **That explanation is false, and so is
the advice drawn from it.**

```console
$ git rev-list --merges 5559679229..e126687a9a | wc -l
0
```

The range is **fully linear**, so there is no merge topology to reorder anything.
And the underlying order is not merely monotone but *strictly* so: committer
timestamps (`%ct`) increase across all 290 entries with zero inversions, and
across these forty in particular. What inverts at 95 and 116 is only the rendered
local-date **string** (`%cd`), a timezone artefact. `dd856e48bb` is
`2026-08-06T20:40:47-07:00`, which is `2026-08-07T03:40:47Z` and absolutely later
than the `2026-08-07T00:05:03+00:00` before it.

So the check the first draft discarded is real, and **stronger** than the
positional one it substituted: **compare `%ct`, never `%cd`.** Absolute-time
monotonicity holds over the whole queue and is the cheapest ordering check there
is. The positional check below is sound and is kept, now as a second check rather
than a replacement. The tranche spans 2026-08-05 to 2026-08-10 in rendered local
dates, which is exactly the field that must not be compared.

**8 of 40 is 20%.** Tranche 1 gave 6 of 40, or 15%. #2524's stratified sample of
63 gave 11, or 17.5%. **The three are close, and that closeness is the only
reason any of them is worth quoting.**

Applied to the 290: **roughly 50-60 real gaps. This is an estimate derived from
three samples totalling 143 entries. It is not a count of anything.** #2611
records ~51 from #2524's rate; tranche 1 pulled that slightly down and this one
pulls it slightly back up, which is what three samples agreeing to within five
percentage points looks like.

**Four reasons not to treat 20% as the rate**, stated inline because the number
will be quoted:

1. This is a contiguous slice of commit history, not a random draw, so a period
   effect and a sample rate are not separable here.
2. Four of the eight gaps are in two subsystems — the parsers (`[107]`, `[114]`,
   `[118]`) and priority scheduling (`[81]`, `[91]`). A tranche whose commits
   cluster differently would come back differently.
3. Two of #2524's eleven worked gaps (`[91]`, `[118]`) fall inside this range, so
   the samples overlap in population even though they share no entry, and this
   tranche's 8 includes both.
4. Eight of forty is small enough that two entries landing either way moves the
   figure from 15% to 25%.

**What is a count, and holds regardless:** these forty contain eight real gaps
and thirty-two labels that contradict the record.
